1. The favicon.ico file

Right now your icon exists in one place: assets/favicon.svg.

Google's crawler, out of habit, also looks for a file called favicon.ico sitting at the front door of your site — literally solaremicalculator.pages.dev/favicon.ico. That file doesn't exist, so if Google looks there first, it finds nothing.

What I'd do: make a second copy of your same gold-sun-on-blue icon, in the older .ico format, and put it at the front door. Then add one line to index.html pointing at it.

What changes for you: nothing visible today. It just gives Google two places to find your icon instead of one. Your existing SVG stays exactly as it is — browsers keep using that.

Honest expectation: this does not make the globe disappear tomorrow. Google still has to come back and look, which takes days to weeks. This is "remove the excuse," not "fix it now."

2. The site name block

Look at the screenshot again — above your blue title it says Cloudflare.

That's supposed to be your site's name. Google is guessing, and it guessed wrong, because pages.dev belongs to Cloudflare and you're a guest on their address.

What I'd do: add a small hidden block of text in the page's <head> — invisible to visitors — that tells Google directly: "this site is called Solar Loan EMI Calculator." It's the same kind of thing as the two structured-data blocks you already have on lines 43 and 63.

What changes for you: if Google accepts it, that "Cloudflare" line becomes "Solar Loan EMI Calculator." Nothing on the visible page changes at all.

Honest expectation: Google treats this as a suggestion, not a command. On a shared subdomain like yours it often ignores it and keeps showing the domain owner. Buying solaremicalculator.in is what actually solves this properly. But the block costs nothing and sometimes works.
