Sure. Here's the simplest way to picture it.

Google has a program called a crawler — a robot that wanders the internet, opens web pages, reads them, and adds them to Google's search list. When your site goes live, that robot will show up at your door.

These two files are notes you leave on the door for that robot.

robots.txt — "what you're allowed to look at"

This is the permission note.

Your file says exactly two useful things:

- User-agent: * — "this note is for every search robot, not just Google"
- Allow: / — "you may read the whole site, nothing here is private"

That's it. That's the whole rule.

Some websites use this file to block robots — a shopping site might say "don't look at the checkout page." You have nothing to hide (it's one public calculator), so yours just says "go ahead, read everything."

The last line points at your other file: "by the way, the list of my pages is over there."

sitemap.xml — "here is a list of my pages"

This is the table of contents.

Instead of making the robot wander around guessing what pages exist, you hand it a list. Right now your list has exactly one page on it — your calculator — because that's all you have.

Reading your file line by line:

┌──────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│     Line     │                      Plain English                      │
├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ <loc>        │ The page's address                                      │
├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ <lastmod>    │ When you last meaningfully changed it (31 July)         │
├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ <changefreq> │ "I update this about monthly" — a hint, not a promise   │
├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ <priority>   │ How important this page is compared to your other pages │
└──────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A note on priority: it's 1.0 (the highest) but with only one page that number does nothing — it's comparing the page against itself. It starts mattering when you have five pages and want to say "the calculator matters more than the About page."

Do you actually need them?

Honestly: not really, not for one page. Google would find and index your site fine without either file. I wrote them anyway for one specific reason —

When you sign up for Google Search Console (the free tool that shows you what people typed into Google to find your site — you'll want this), it asks you to submit a sitemap. Having the file ready means that step is already done. And it's the standard, expected setup, so it costs nothing to have.

The one thing you must remember

Both files contain a fake address: solaremicalculator.pages.dev. I made that up — it's your planned name, but Cloudflare hasn't actually given it to you yet.

Once Cloudflare Pages is connected and tells you your real address, that address has to be corrected in both files — one line in robots.txt (line 18), one line in sitemap.xml (line 20). If it's wrong, you're handing the robot a note pointing at a house that doesn't exist.
